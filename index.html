Run this for a client fleet

The fleet above is a demonstration. To run the same view on a client’s fleet we need four inputs for each group of vehicles:

  1. Vehicle class and fuel. For example light truck, diesel.
  2. Annual litres. Per vehicle, and the number of vehicles in the group.
  3. State and region. Metro, regional or remote.
  4. Electrifiable share. The portion of the group that could realistically switch.

We return this page with the client’s fleet loaded. Electrification cost comparisons are added once client electricity tariffs and charging arrangements are known.

Method

Show how wholesale, excise, pump price, margin, fleet and electricity cost are built
  • Wholesale. Net wholesale W = TGP - 1.1 × E, weekly (Friday) log changes, moving-block bootstrap, block weeks chosen from the autocorrelation of squared changes, paths × weeks, seed . One block start is drawn per block and the same rows are used for all 14 city-fuel series, so joint movement is preserved as it occurred.
  • 2026-style shock. The cumulative price path is scaled by a multiplier, at the top setting. That is the smallest multiplier at which the simulated 95th percentile of the largest 12-week Sydney diesel rise equals the actual 2026 rise, so a 2026-sized rise corresponds to the worst 12 weeks of a 1-in-20 year.
  • Excise. Held at c/L (official rate from 3 Aug 2026). Never simulated. Changing the excise field changes the simulated weeks only, not the history the budget price is built from.
  • Pump. P(h) = M + Σk phik TGP(h-k) over lags 0 to 4, phi normalised to sum to 1. Estimated from the NSW, QLD and WA station panels.
  • Margin M. 2026-window margins by state × fuel × region class, estimated on calm weeks. The narrower rising-wholesale margin that pumps show while they lag a run-up is not applied, so simulated pump prices keep the wider calm-week margin.
  • Fleet. S(h) = Σv (Lv/52) × Pv(h). Budget is the same litres at the latest pump price.
  • Fuel-price exposure avoided. The overrun on the fuel the fleet still buys, measured against the budget for that fuel. Switched vehicles leave both the spend and the budget, so the result does not depend on the electricity price or on any electricity saving. With “Electricity follows fuel” above zero, the electricity cost moves with the fleet fuel price and is added.

Data coverage

Show the sources, date windows and state coverage
  • AIP terminal gate prices, daily 2 Jan 2004 to , no gaps, 7 capitals × petrol and diesel.
  • Station panels cover 2022 and 2026 only. There is no data for 2023 to 2025 and nothing is bridged across that gap.
  • Station data exists for NSW, QLD and WA only. Melbourne, Adelaide, Darwin and Hobart use a three-state Major Cities proxy margin, marked “proxy” in the pass-through table.
  • QLD data ends 31 Aug 2026. WA 2026 uses the pre-expansion station set.

Limitations

Show what is excluded and where the numbers are weakest
  • NSW diesel and premium diesel are never used. NSW publishes at a 299.9 c/L ceiling and 17% of diesel station-days in March to April 2026 sit at it, so the surviving mean is biased down. Sydney diesel therefore uses a QLD and WA proxy margin.
  • The metro petrol price cycle is not modelled. Weekly figures for city petrol are only reliable as 12-week averages; single weeks can be 7 to 8 c/L off.
  • Remote margins are window-dependent, 8 to 10 c/L wider in 2026 than 2022, which is why the remote-area margin is an input here. Range on the two windows: .
  • Premium diesel has no wholesale series, so it is priced off the diesel terminal gate price; its margin absorbs the difference. The demo fleet does not use it.
  • Electrification costs are not estimated. Electricity tariffs, charging arrangements and vehicle eligibility are client inputs. Fuel tax credits are not modelled. Licence terms for the state price feeds are unchecked for commercial use.
